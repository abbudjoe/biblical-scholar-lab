## 1. Purpose

DR-06 defined the Translation Nuance Core as a custom semantic architecture at the system level and approved a staged `A0–A6` path for possible learned architectural specialization. DR-11 defined the foundation-model candidates and required the baseline to preserve each official model's tokenizer and neural topology.

DR-12 defines how those two decisions become one model-facing architecture.

Biblical Scholar Lab needs more than a language model that receives several translations in a prompt and writes a plausible comparison. It needs a system that can:

- resolve the exact source, edition, passage, translation, and lineage identities involved;
- retrieve reviewed source-language, alignment, linguistic, textual, versional, and scholarly evidence;
- represent several compatible or competing causes of a translation difference;
- distinguish upstream textual state, source-language construal, transfer operation, target-language constraint, revision lineage, and interpretive effect;
- ask the foundation model to perform bounded comparison and explanation over that evidence;
- verify the model's structured claims against the evidence packet and authoritative graph;
- preserve uncertainty, disagreement, rights, and provenance;
- promote model-generated analyses only through an explicit review process;
- test whether learned specialization adds value beyond the structured system baseline;
- add auxiliary heads, adapters, graph memory, specialist models, or core modifications only when a measured representational deficit justifies them.

DR-12 therefore defines:

- the Translation Nuance semantic execution stack;
- the authority boundary between deterministic structure, learned inference, and reviewed scholarship;
- the model-facing evidence-packet and output contracts;
- structure-first runtime behavior;
- the `A0–A6` implementation and experiment ladder;
- family-specific integration constraints;
- multi-model routing and escalation;
- multilingual, multimodal, long-context, rights, and reproducibility behavior;
- training-facing objectives and negative controls;
- promotion gates, ablations, benchmark requirements, and hard failures.

DR-12 does **not** select the winning foundation model, choose storage products, authorize a custom foundation block, approve a billable training campaign, or define final loss weights and thresholds.

## 2. Governing principle

> **The authoritative Translation Nuance record remains external, versioned, evidence-bearing, and reviewable. The model may retrieve, compare, propose, explain, and challenge Translation Nuance analyses; it may not silently become the source of truth for textual identity, alignment, lineage, diagnosis, evidence, or scholarly consensus. Learned specialization must improve a fixed structured-system baseline under controlled ablation before it may become part of the product architecture.**

The system preserves this chain:

```text
reviewed source and scholarly evidence
    → authoritative Translation Nuance graph and linguistic layers
    → deterministic semantic execution and rights filtering
    → versioned model evidence packet
    → model proposal or explanation
    → structured validation and claim/evidence audit
    → reviewed operational answer
    → optional candidate promotion through explicit human or approved adjudication
```

No model output may bypass that chain merely because it is fluent, confident, generated by a larger model, or repeated across several models.

## 3. Architecture overview

The Translation Nuance model integration has six logical planes.

### 3.1 Authoritative evidence plane

This plane contains the approved, versioned records defined in DR-04 through DR-10:

- canon, reference, and versification identities;
- textual-history and provenance graph;
- Translation Nuance entities and causal ontology;
- linguistic annotations and competing analyses;
- ancient-version and apparatus evidence;
- scholarship, claims, citations, and landscape assessments;
- rights, access, lineage, and release decisions.

This plane is authoritative for what evidence and reviewed assertions the project currently holds. It may contain disagreement and uncertainty. "Authoritative" does not mean every scholarly assertion is true; it means this is the authoritative record of the assertion, its evidence, status, and provenance.

### 3.2 Translation Nuance Semantic Kernel

DR-12 formally introduces the `TranslationNuanceSemanticKernel`, abbreviated `TNSK`.

The TNSK is the project's custom semantic execution layer. It is **not**:

- a CUDA or GPU kernel;
- a transformer block;
- a hidden learned database;
- an autonomous scholarly judge;
- a substitute for expert review.

It is a deterministic or explicitly rule-governed library/service that performs invariant operations over the authoritative evidence plane.

Its minimum responsibilities are:

```text
resolve comparison identities
validate source and target revision compatibility
materialize reviewed alignments and alternatives
classify available evidence by role and proximity
apply causal-ontology compatibility rules
validate causal-chain topology
track lineage and evidence dependence
identify required but missing evidentiary layers
apply rights and disclosure filters
compile model evidence packets
validate model-produced structured analyses
record omissions, warnings, and unresolved conflicts
```

The TNSK supplies semantic constraints and auditability. It does not decide a contested translation diagnosis without an approved rule or reviewed assertion.

### 3.3 Evidence compilation plane

The `TranslationNuanceEvidenceCompiler` selects the minimum sufficient, rights-authorized subgraph and related text spans for a defined request.

It produces an immutable `TranslationNuanceEvidencePacket` rather than an untracked prompt assembled from miscellaneous passages.

### 3.4 Model interaction plane

A selected foundation model consumes the packet, invokes approved tools where needed, and produces a structured `TranslationNuanceAnalysisCandidate` plus user-facing prose.

The model may:

- explain a reviewed diagnosis;
- propose candidate diagnoses;
- compare competing analyses;
- identify missing evidence;
- generate translation options with tradeoffs;
- select relevant evidence from an approved packet;
- state uncertainty and request escalation.

The model may not directly mutate authoritative records.

### 3.5 Verification and adjudication plane

The `TranslationNuanceVerifier` checks:

- schema validity;
- identity and revision validity;
- cited-evidence existence;
- claim/evidence compatibility;
- causal-ontology compatibility;
- causal-chain order;
- source-type distinctions;
- intent/effect separation;
- lineage and dependence claims;
- rights and disclosure requirements;
- unsupported certainty;
- contradiction with deterministic tool results.

Deterministic failures block rendering or force a corrected retry. Scholarly disagreements remain visible rather than being automatically "repaired" into one answer.

### 3.6 Presentation and audit plane

The final response is generated from the verified structured analysis and preserves:

- answer depth;
- exact source and edition identities;
- cited evidence;
- material alternatives;
- uncertainties;
- model and tool provenance;
- omissions due to rights, context, or missing evidence;
- escalation or abstention state.

The full audit trace is stored independently of the user-facing prose.

## 4. Authority and trust boundaries

### 4.1 Authoritative records

The following may be authoritative only through the reviewed graph and approved source pipeline:

- source and target identities;
- edition and revision identities;
- exact source text;
- reviewed alignments;
- reviewed translation lineage;
- reviewed apparatus and witness reports;
- rights and access decisions;
- benchmark gold records;
- accepted Translation Nuance diagnoses.

### 4.2 Candidate records

The following remain candidates until explicitly promoted:

- model-generated alignments;
- model-generated causal diagnoses;
- inferred source-base assignments;
- inferred translation lineage;
- model-generated source translations;
- automatic apparatus parses;
- automatically extracted scholarly claims;
- model-generated benchmark items;
- model-generated confidence labels.

### 4.3 Operational selections

For a bounded answer or experiment, the system may select one analysis among several. That selection records:

```text
purpose
scope
selected assertion or candidate
alternatives retained
selection rule
responsible agent or process
supporting evidence
review state
expiration or reconsideration condition
```

An operational selection does not erase competing scholarly records.

### 4.4 Model self-confidence is not authority

A model's token probability, verbal confidence, agreement with another model, or repeated consistency does not establish scholarly correctness.

Model confidence may contribute to routing or calibration only after task-specific calibration is demonstrated and never overrides a deterministic contradiction or missing required evidence.

## 5. Model interaction modes

Every TNC model invocation declares one primary mode.

### `EXPLAIN_REVIEWED_DIAGNOSIS`

The model explains a diagnosis already accepted for the defined scope. It may identify qualifications but may not silently replace the diagnosis.

### `PROPOSE_DIAGNOSIS`

The model proposes one or more candidate causal diagnoses from the supplied evidence. The result remains unreviewed.

### `COMPARE_DIAGNOSES`

The model compares reviewed or candidate analyses, identifies differing assumptions and evidence, and may state which is better supported under an identified method.

### `AUDIT_TRANSLATION_CLAIM`

The model evaluates a user's, commentary's, or prior model's claim about why translations differ.

### `GENERATE_TRANSLATION_OPTIONS`

The model generates one or more new renderings, each labeled as model-generated and accompanied by stated tradeoffs and source construal.

### `EXPLAIN_TRANSLATION_LINEAGE`

The model explains revision relationships and dependence without treating file count or wording similarity as independent evidence.

### `IDENTIFY_MISSING_EVIDENCE`

The model identifies what evidence is required before a responsible diagnosis can be made.

### `PAGE_TO_TRANSLATION_ANALYSIS`

The model begins from an image-derived candidate, but exact text, edition, and passage identity must be resolved before a scholarly diagnosis is rendered.

A single user request may require several internal invocations, but each invocation remains typed and auditable.

## 6. Translation Nuance Evidence Packet

The `TranslationNuanceEvidencePacket`, abbreviated `TNEP`, is the normative model-facing evidence unit.

A TNEP is immutable and content-addressed. It binds the exact graph, sources, tools, policies, and compilation decisions used for one analysis.

### 6.0 Normative logical record

```text
TranslationNuanceEvidencePacket
  packet_id
  packet_schema_revision
  packet_revision
  packet_content_hash
  request_id
  interaction_mode
  creation_activity_id
  graph_snapshot_id
  rights_snapshot_id
  review_snapshot_id
  experiment_or_runtime_context
  task_intent
  question_language
  answer_language
  canon_profile_id
  reference_scheme_ids[]
  passage_selection_id
  comparison_frame_id
  source_representation_revisions[]
  target_realization_revisions[]
  textual_state_assertions[]
  linguistic_analysis_sets[]
  alignment_set_revisions[]
  difference_unit_revisions[]
  causal_diagnosis_revisions[]
  causal_chain_revisions[]
  translation_lineage_assertions[]
  ancient_version_and_apparatus_evidence[]
  scholarship_claims_and_evidence[]
  competing_analyses[]
  counterevidence[]
  required_abstentions[]
  unresolved_questions[]
  citation_handles[]
  rights_and_display_constraints[]
  leakage_and_split_constraints[]
  selection_trace
  truncation_and_omission_report
```

Packet invariants are:

1. Every record points to an exact immutable revision.
2. Every textual span identifies the exact text view and coordinate system.
3. Every cause label identifies the approved DR-06 ontology revision.
4. Every scholarly claim identifies its source and evidential role.
5. Every material conflict or uncertainty remains explicit.
6. Every omission caused by rights, evidence, or context limits appears in the omission report.
7. Every displayed quotation has a rights and provenance decision.
8. No model-generated candidate appears as accepted evidence without an explicit promotion record.
9. The packet is independently validatable without loading the language model.

### 6.1 Required identity fields

```text
packet_id
packet_schema_revision
packet_content_hash
request_id
interaction_mode
graph_snapshot_id
rights_snapshot_id
compiler_version
compilation_policy_id
created_at
```

### 6.2 Comparison frame

The packet identifies:

```text
work and passage identities
canon and reference schemes
source textual form and edition revisions
target translation works and edition revisions
source and target languages
question and answer languages
comparison purpose
requested answer depth
```

### 6.3 Source-textual-state evidence

The packet may include:

- exact source passages;
- alternate source readings;
- critical-edition and apparatus reports;
- ancient-version compatibility evidence;
- source-base assignments;
- textual-form differences;
- unresolved source uncertainty.

### 6.4 Linguistic evidence

The packet may include reviewed or competing:

- segmentation;
- lemma and morphology;
- syntax;
- sense assignments;
- semantic roles;
- referents and coreference;
- discourse and pragmatic analyses;
- prosodic or cantillation evidence;
- annotation provenance and disagreement.

### 6.5 Alignment and difference evidence

The packet may include:

- reviewed and candidate alignment sets;
- exact source and target span selectors;
- translation difference units;
- materiality classifications;
- comparison-frame constraints;
- alignment confidence dimensions;
- unresolved or alternative alignments.

### 6.6 Translation and revision lineage

The packet may include:

- translation work, edition, and instance identities;
- revision and predecessor relations;
- source-edition declarations;
- terminology policies;
- translation-technique profiles;
- dependence and independence assertions;
- relevant translator documentation.

### 6.7 Causal-diagnosis evidence

The packet may include:

- reviewed diagnoses;
- candidate diagnoses;
- cause-axis labels;
- causal roles;
- ordered chains;
- evidence and counterevidence;
- missing-evidence requirements;
- intent/effect distinctions;
- review state.

### 6.8 Scholarly evidence

The packet may include:

- exact source spans;
- bibliographic identity;
- methodology and perspective;
- publication status;
- source dependence;
- claim/evidence roles;
- translated-quotation provenance;
- landscape-assessment scope.

### 6.9 Rights and disclosure envelope

The packet declares:

- which components may be supplied to the selected model;
- which spans may be shown to the user;
- which content may be retained;
- which content is metadata-only;
- which content requires paraphrase rather than quotation;
- which omissions are rights-driven;
- which artifact lineage the output inherits.

### 6.10 Completeness and omission report

The packet must state:

```text
required evidence present
required evidence missing
optional evidence omitted
context-budget omissions
rights-driven omissions
conflicting records retained
unsupported operations disabled
```

The model may not infer that omitted evidence does not exist.

## 7. Packet serialization and renderers

The normative TNEP is a typed logical object. The first implementation must provide at least three versioned projections.

### 7.1 Canonical audit projection

A deterministic JSON projection is required for review, hashing, tests, and artifact exchange.

It must use:

- stable IDs;
- explicit schema revision;
- deterministic ordering;
- explicit null, unknown, and omitted states;
- evidence references rather than copied conclusions where possible;
- no unversioned free-text fields standing in for structured identity.

DR-28 will finalize repository-wide canonicalization details.

### 7.2 Human inspection projection

A readable Markdown or HTML projection presents the packet to reviewers without changing its semantics.

### 7.3 Model context projection

A compact, model-family-aware renderer may use tagged text, constrained JSON, or another approved representation.

The renderer must preserve:

- identity;
- causal role;
- evidence links;
- uncertainty;
- alternatives;
- source language;
- rights omissions.

A renderer is a derived view. It cannot become the authoritative packet.

### 7.4 Tensor and graph projection

An approved auxiliary component may receive a derived tensor or graph projection containing:

- source and target span embeddings;
- relation-type identities;
- graph topology;
- evidence, alternative, review, and rights masks;
- language, passage, and translation-lineage identifiers;
- confidence dimensions;
- source/target pairing and causal-role information.

The projection records its exact encoder, ordering, padding, truncation, packing, and content hash. Model subword boundaries do not become scholarly span boundaries.

### 7.5 Renderer invariance

The project must test whether material answers change merely because the same packet is rendered differently.

Where model families exhibit materially different structured-input behavior, family-specific renderers are allowed, but they must represent the same logical packet and be compared under a renderer-ablation test.

### 8.1 Diagnosis candidate

Each diagnosis contains:

```text

causal_role

epistemic status

```

### 8.2 Causal chains

A causal chain preserves order, for example:

```text
same source text
→ syntactic ambiguity
→ alternate construals
→ one rendering resolves the ambiguity
→ different interpretive effect
```

The model may not flatten that chain into one label.

### 8.3 Structure-first generation

For scholarly and benchmark modes, the structured candidate is generated and validated before final prose.

Constrained decoding may enforce syntactic schema compliance, but schema-valid output is not assumed semantically correct. Structured-generation methods must therefore be evaluated for both compliance and answer quality.[^structured-output]

### 8.4 Invalid structured output

An invalid candidate may be retried under the same approved model and evidence identity. Repair machinery may correct formatting, but it may not silently add or remove scholarly claims.

Repeated invalidity is a model or harness failure and may trigger escalation.

## 9. Translation Nuance claim ledger

Every final material statement maps to a claim-ledger entry derived from DR-02 and DR-09.

The ledger records:

```text
claim_id
claim type
claim text or structured proposition
epistemic status
method
source and target scope
supporting and counterevidence
TNC cause and causal role
source role and proximity
translation provenance
review state
model or human origin
rendered sentence span
```

The user-facing answer may be concise. The audit system must still be able to answer:

- Which evidence supports this sentence?
- Is the claim textual, linguistic, translational, historical, or theological?
- Is it reviewed or model-generated?
- Which alternatives were omitted from the prose?
- Was a source unavailable because of rights or context budget?

This ledger is evidence rationale, not private chain-of-thought.

## 10. TNSK semantic validations

The TNSK implements an explicit compatibility and prerequisite matrix.

Examples include:

### 10.1 Textual-state claims

`DIFFERENT_SOURCE_READING` requires suitable textual or source-compatibility evidence. Surface difference among modern translations is insufficient.

### 10.2 Ancient-version claims

A different ancient-version wording cannot become an exact source reconstruction without the DR-08 retroversion requirements.

### 10.3 Target-language constraints

A `TARGET_LANGUAGE_CONSTRAINT` diagnosis requires evidence about the target language and the actual construction. General model fluency is not sufficient review evidence.

### 10.4 Confessional or interpretive influence

`CONFESSIONAL_TRADITION_INFLUENCE` requires passage-relevant translator documentation, revision history, or qualified scholarly analysis. Publisher or tradition identity alone is insufficient.

### 10.5 Intent and effect

An `INTERPRETIVE_EFFECT` may be observed from the wording. `TRANSLATOR_INTENT` requires separate historical evidence.

### 10.6 Lineage independence

Several translation instances from one edition cannot be counted independently. Several editions in one revision family may remain dependent.

### 10.7 Alignment

Alignment does not establish equivalence, materiality, or causal explanation by itself.

### 10.8 Causal-chain order

A target-language realization cannot be represented as causing an upstream manuscript reading. The kernel rejects impossible or reversed chain order.

### 10.9 Missing evidence

When a diagnosis requires an unavailable layer, the validator returns `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` rather than allowing the model to fill the gap.

The compatibility matrix is a versioned design artifact. Sol may implement its execution mechanics but may not alter its scholarly rules without design review.

## 11. Runtime diagnostic workflow

The default runtime sequence is:

1. Classify scope, risk, interaction mode, and answer depth.
2. Resolve canon, reference, work, textual form, editions, and translations.
3. Establish the comparison frame.
4. Determine whether the source textual state is the same, different, or unresolved.
5. Retrieve exact source and target realizations.
6. Retrieve or compute candidate alignments under approved methods.
7. Retrieve linguistic analyses and alternatives.
8. Retrieve translation lineage, policy, and technique evidence.
9. Retrieve ancient-version, apparatus, and scholarly evidence where relevant.
10. Run TNSK prerequisite, dependence, and rights checks.
11. Compile and hash the TNEP.
12. Ask the model to produce a structured candidate.
13. Validate the candidate.
14. Request bounded correction, abstention, or model escalation where required.
15. Render the answer and claim ledger.
16. Store the full audit identity.

The model may request additional approved tools during steps 12–14. Every call is logged and added to a revised packet or evidence supplement.

## 12. Tool-use contract

The model interacts with TNC evidence through typed tools rather than direct database queries.

Minimum logical operations include:

```text
resolve_comparison_frame
get_source_textual_state
get_translation_realizations
get_alignment_sets
get_linguistic_analyses
get_translation_lineage
get_ancient_version_evidence
get_apparatus_evidence
get_scholarly_evidence
get_reviewed_diagnoses
validate_diagnosis
compare_diagnoses
compile_translation_nuance_packet
```

Tool results identify exact revisions, rights, coverage, alternatives, and warnings.

Tool-use training may teach when and how to call these operations. Tool outputs themselves remain the source of exact facts; the model does not memorize a tool result as a permanent authoritative update.

Tool-using language-model research supports the general proposition that models can be trained to decide when to call external APIs and incorporate the results, but our tools remain project-defined and evidence-governed.[^toolformer]

## 13. A0 — Structured-system baseline

`A0` is mandatory and must be measured before any learned TNC architectural extension.

It consists of:

```text
unmodified official foundation-model topology
+ authoritative TNC evidence graph
+ TNSK semantic kernel
+ deterministic tools
+ TNEP compiler
+ structure-first output
+ verifier and claim ledger
+ ordinary prompting or minimal task demonstrations
```

### 13.1 A0 purpose

A0 answers:

- How much capability already exists in the foundation model?
- How much is supplied by better evidence and structure?
- Which failures are retrieval, packet, capacity, or reasoning failures?
- Is domain training necessary?
- Does the TNSK reduce source-type and evidence errors?

### 13.2 Required A0 ablations

At minimum:

```text
raw passages only
raw passages + ordinary RAG
TNC evidence packet without semantic validation
TNC evidence packet + TNSK validation
TNC packet + tools + verifier
```

The same model and question set must be used where technically possible.

### 13.3 A0 is a product-capable architecture

A0 is not merely a temporary prototype. It may remain the deployed architecture if it reaches the product threshold without weight adaptation.

Retrieval-augmented generation provides a general precedent for combining parametric generation with explicit non-parametric evidence and provenance.[^rag]

## 15. A2 — Auxiliary heads

`A2` is the first approved category of neural-topology extension, but no particular head is authorized for implementation until an A1 result meets the escalation gate.

Candidate head contracts include:

### `SpanAlignmentHead`

Produces sparse source-target span links and confidence over a defined comparison frame.

### `CauseClassificationHead`

Produces multi-label cause-axis candidates for a Translation Difference Unit.

### `CausalRoleHead`

Classifies causes as upstream, proximate, contributing, competing, or unresolved.

### `EvidenceFitnessHead`

Scores whether a typed evidence item can support, qualify, contradict, or fail to support a typed claim.

### `LineageRelationHead`

Proposes revision, predecessor, source, or dependence relations.

### `CalibrationAndAbstentionHead`

Predicts whether the system has sufficient evidence and whether escalation is required.

### 15.1 Head authority

Head outputs remain model-generated candidates. They cannot update authoritative TNC records automatically.

### 15.2 Training-only by default

An auxiliary head should initially be treated as training-only or diagnostic unless inference use produces measured benefit.

### 15.3 Family-specific implementation

Hidden-state locations, pooled representations, and target modules may differ across Qwen, Gemma, and Ministral. The logical output contracts remain shared; implementation is family-specific.

### 15.4 Representation-tap contract

A head consumes one or more versioned `RepresentationTap` outputs:

```text
RepresentationTap
  model_artifact_id
  component_id
  layer_or_block_id
  pre_or_post_normalization
  token_or_span_pooling_method
  modality_state
  gradient_policy
  precision
  content_hash
```

A bounded probe experiment selects candidate taps. The project does not assume that the same absolute or relative layers are optimal across model families.

### 15.5 Span and relation behavior

Span heads use DR-07 selectors and an approved pooling method. They must support many-to-many, discontinuous, and null alignment. A one-to-one token matrix is not a sufficient universal alignment architecture.

Cause heads are multi-label and hierarchical. They separately predict cause axis, cause label, and causal-chain role rather than compressing the entire diagnosis into one class.

Evidence and calibration heads report task-specific support and abstention states; they may not emit one global confidence number that conceals missing evidence.

### 15.6 Parent behavior and retention

Each head is classified as:

```text
TRAINING_ONLY
OPTIONAL_RUNTIME_DIAGNOSTIC
REQUIRED_RUNTIME_COMPONENT
```

With a runtime head disabled, the approved parent generation path must be restored within the defined tolerance. A retained head must justify its latency, calibration, quantization, and deployment cost.

### 15.7 Required ablation

Each head is evaluated against an A1 model with the same data, training budget, and base checkpoint. Head metrics and downstream explanation metrics must both improve; classification improvement alone is insufficient.

## 16. A3 — Relation-aware adapters

`A3` introduces reversible, modular learned components without rewriting foundation blocks.

Candidate forms include:

- TNC-specific LoRA modules;
- relation embeddings;
- span-pair adapters;
- passage-pair cross-attention adapters;
- adapters selected by task or evidence type;
- separate compact and scholarly-mode adapter stacks.

Low-rank adaptation provides a general mechanism for freezing base weights and injecting trainable low-rank updates, making it useful for reversible, role-specific experiments.[^lora] Knowledge-specific adapters provide a second precedent for adding modular domain representations without replacing the underlying model.[^kadapter]

### 16.1 Preferred first A3 architecture

The preferred first A3 experiment is a **relation-aware residual adapter** containing:

- packet-derived relation embeddings;
- source and target span representations;
- a low-parameter transformation or bounded cross-attention module;
- a gated residual contribution at selected family-specific layers;
- an exact disable path.

The initial runtime modes are:

```text
TNC_ADAPTER_OFF
TNC_ADAPTER_LIGHT
TNC_ADAPTER_FULL
```

The first router is deterministic from the approved interaction and complexity class. Learned routing is a later ablation.

### 16.2 Relation embeddings

Relation embeddings may represent approved handles for:

- alignment relation;
- difference scope;
- cause axis and cause type;
- causal-chain role;
- evidence role;
- source type;
- review state;
- translation-lineage relation.

They may not embed restricted raw text without an approved lineage and release decision.

### 16.3 Adapter constraints

- The unmerged adapter remains the scientific master.
- Merged weights are a separate derivative.
- Adapter routing is explicit and recorded.
- Different family adapters are not assumed portable.
- The adapter targets exact model components and revisions.
- Disabling the adapter restores the approved parent path within tolerance.
- An adapter may not carry restricted lineage into an open model without DR-10 review.
- Adapter benefit must exceed an A1 control under matched data and compute.
- Quantization, multilingual, multimodal, and long-context retention are required before promotion.

## 17. A4 — Sidecar graph encoder or structured memory

`A4` is considered when evidence shows that plain-text or structured-JSON serialization loses relational information that matters to Translation Nuance.

A sidecar may encode a query-scoped TNC subgraph and provide representations to the foundation model through:

- cross-attention;
- prefix or memory tokens;
- an external memory interface;
- retrieved relation packets;
- iterative graph-query tools.

### 17.1 The graph remains external truth

The sidecar representation is a derived, versioned projection. It does not replace the authoritative graph.

### 17.2 Required comparison

A4 must be compared against:

```text
same subgraph serialized as text/JSON
same graph exposed through tools
same evidence token budget
same foundation model and training data
```

### 17.3 Graph construction

The authoritative graph is built from reviewed DR-04 through DR-10 records. An LLM-derived graph index may be used as a candidate discovery aid but not as an unquestioned truth layer.

Graph-based retrieval can improve corpus-level sensemaking in some settings, but its value for Translation Nuance must be tested against our typed, provenance-bearing graph and local passage tasks rather than assumed from generic GraphRAG results.[^graphrag]

### 17.4 Preferred first A4 architecture

The preferred first sidecar is:

```text
bounded query-scoped TNC subgraph
    → relation-aware graph encoder
    → typed memory vectors
    → gated cross-attention from selected language-model layers
```

The subgraph is selected under exact passage, rights, evidence-quality, leakage, node/edge, and latency limits. The foundation model remains independently runnable without the sidecar.

### 17.5 Structured-memory contract

Each memory vector records:

```text
memory_vector_id
source_node_or_edge_handles[]
relation_type
scope
provenance
confidence_dimensions
rights_mask
review_state_mask
encoder_revision
```

Every model claim using graph memory must resolve back to the underlying handles.

### 17.6 Isolation and alternatives

The first A4 experiment freezes the foundation blocks and trains only the graph encoder, projector or cross-attention modules, and any approved heads. Joint full-model training requires a later decision.

Controlled alternatives may include:

```text
graph encoder + prefix memory
graph encoder + soft prompt
graph retrieval + verbalized paths
tool-mediated iterative graph query
rectangular or sparse knowledge attention
```

Work such as KBLaM, G-Retriever, and GNN-RAG provides useful precedents for structured memory, bounded graph retrieval, and specialist graph reasoning, but none is adopted without a matched project-specific ablation.[^kblam][^gretriever][^gnnrag]

## 18. A5 — Specialist model or tiered system

`A5` separates bounded specialist operations from the main generative model.

Candidate specialist roles include:

- source-target aligner;
- Translation Difference Unit detector;
- cause classifier;
- evidence reranker;
- lineage resolver;
- citation-entailment verifier;
- target-language constraint classifier.

A specialist may be:

- deterministic;
- an encoder;
- a small classifier;
- a sequence-to-sequence model;
- another foundation model operating under a typed contract.

The main model remains responsible for integrated explanation unless the approved design changes.

A tiered system is an approved success outcome when it is more accurate, inspectable, or economical than embedding every capability in one model.

## 19. A6 — Core foundation-model modification

`A6` includes changes such as:

- new relation-aware foundation blocks;
- modified attention or DeltaNet recurrence;
- embedded graph-neural layers;
- specialized experts or routers inside the foundation model;
- tokenizer or embedding surgery;
- altered multimodal positional machinery.

DR-12 does not authorize A6 implementation.

A6 requires:

1. a persistent benchmarked deficit after A0–A5-appropriate interventions;
2. evidence that the deficit is representational rather than primarily data, retrieval, supervision, capacity, or evaluation failure;
3. a falsifiable architectural hypothesis;
4. a separate approved design review;
5. a controlled ablation;
6. compatibility, quantization, deployment, multimodal, multilingual, and reproducibility analysis;
7. owner approval and a separate budget.

## 20. Escalation and promotion gates

Advancement beyond A0 or A1 requires all of the following:

### 20.1 Persistent material deficit

The baseline fails a product-critical Translation Nuance capability at a rate that exceeds the approved threshold.

### 20.2 Simpler interventions exhausted

The failure persists after appropriate improvements to:

- source data;
- evidence packet construction;
- tool results;
- retrieval;
- model capacity;
- A1 supervision;
- prompting and structured output;
- verification.

### 20.3 Representational hypothesis

There is evidence that the model loses or cannot reliably manipulate the relation structure, rather than merely lacking a fact.

### 20.4 Matched ablation

The proposed extension is compared under the same:

- checkpoint;
- data;
- graph snapshot;
- training tokens;
- evaluation;
- tool access;
- context budget;
- reasoning mode;
- compute accounting.

### 20.5 Net value

The gain must justify any regression or cost in:

- general capability;
- ancient-language fidelity;
- multilingual behavior;
- multimodality;
- long context;
- training stability;
- inference latency;
- mobile conversion;
- quantization;
- maintainability;
- release complexity.

Exact numerical gates are defined in the benchmark and experiment reviews.

## 21. Foundation-family integration constraints

### 21.1 Qwen family

The baseline must preserve the official hybrid Gated DeltaNet/full-attention architecture, multimodal positional machinery, tokenizer, processor, and active kernel path recorded under DR-11.

Any A2 or A3 implementation must identify how it interacts with DeltaNet and attention layers and must not assume a conventional all-attention stack.

### 21.2 Gemma 4 12B Unified

Because the 12B model does not expose a separate vision tower in the same way as the other candidates, TNC adaptation and auxiliary components require a distinct multimodal-retention experiment.

### 21.3 Gemma 4 31B

The 31B Base model may provide a clean large-capacity reference. Its component structure differs from 12B Unified and cannot inherit the same freeze policy by name alone.

### 21.4 Ministral 3

Tokenizer, processor, vision encoder, Base/Instruct/Reasoning lineage, and edge deployment behavior remain explicit. A reasoning checkpoint is not treated as equivalent to an Instruct checkpoint with a longer output budget.

### 21.5 No lowest-common-denominator architecture

A common TNC logical contract does not require suppressing family-native strengths. Family-specific renderers, adapters, and preservation strategies are permitted where the underlying evidence and output semantics remain comparable.

## 22. Multi-model routing and escalation

The architecture may route cases among compact, large, specialist, or multimodal components.

Routing may use deterministic case-complexity features such as:

```text
number of textual forms
number and depth of competing alignments
number of cause axes implicated
unresolved linguistic analyses
evidence-packet size
number of scholarly disputes
ancient-version retroversion requirement
cross-language count
image uncertainty
validator failure history
```

Routing may not rely solely on the generating model's self-reported confidence.

The large model remains subject to the same TNEP and verifier. Escalation does not relax evidence rules.

The router and thresholds are finalized in DR-16 and the benchmark/experiment reviews.

## 23. Multilingual behavior

TNC identities and cause labels are language-neutral. Surface text and quotations preserve their actual language.

Every model invocation separately identifies:

```text
source language
target translation language
question language
answer language
evidence-source languages
published translation languages
model-generated pivot languages
```

The system must disclose hidden pivots and may not use English-only reasoning as though it were native-language scholarship.

Translation Nuance training and evaluation must include:

- native-language cases;
- parallel cross-language cases;
- target-language constraint cases;
- answer-language fidelity;
- multilingual citation and quotation provenance;
- per-language regression reports.

## 24. Multimodal behavior

Page images can generate candidates for:

- edition and translation identity;
- passage identity;
- visible wording;
- alignment spans;
- translator notes;
- study notes;
- layout and paratext classes.

Those candidates remain image-derived observations until deterministic text and edition tools verify them.

The image model may not directly create an accepted TNC diagnosis from an unverified screenshot.

Every adapted model must be tested for:

- exact transcription;
- scripture/paratext separation;
- passage resolution;
- translation identification;
- multilingual script handling;
- image-plus-evidence tool use;
- uncertainty under blur, crop, glare, or handwriting.

## 25. Long-context behavior

The full New Testament, a complete book, or several translations may be available as context, but raw long context does not replace a TNEP.

The TNEP provides:

- explicit identities;
- reviewed alignments;
- causal structure;
- evidence roles;
- provenance;
- rights;
- omissions.

Long context supplies broader textual access.

The benchmark must compare:

```text
targeted TNEP only
full book or canon only
TNEP + long context
TNEP + tools + long context
```

Evidence-position sensitivity and distraction remain measured.

## 26. Training curriculum implications

The curriculum should progress from lower-risk structure to integrated reasoning.

A provisional sequence is:

```text
identity and source-type discrimination
→ exact tool use
→ alignment and difference-unit tasks
→ single-axis cause diagnosis
→ multi-axis causal chains
→ evidence and counterevidence selection
→ uncertainty and abstention
→ lineage and target-language constraints
→ ancient-version and apparatus cases
→ multilingual and multimodal cases
→ integrated scholarly explanations
```

Curriculum sequencing is a design variable to be finalized in DR-18. Sol may not reorder stages in a way that changes the experiment without approval.

## 27. Negative controls and adversarial examples

Required negative controls include:

- same meaning with different surface wording;
- materially different meaning with similar wording;
- modern translations from one revision family presented as independent;
- same source text with different construal;
- different source readings with similar target wording;
- ancient-version wording compatible with several sources;
- invented translator intent;
- confessional affiliation without passage evidence;
- target-language constraint that is only stylistic preference;
- alignment that is plausible but not equivalent;
- paratext presented as canonical text;
- model-generated translation presented as published;
- missing evidence disguised by fluent explanation;
- correct answer requiring `UNKNOWN` or `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`;
- misleading retrieved scholarship;
- evidence packet with intentionally reordered or omitted causal stages.

The project must reward correction of false premises, not merely selection among provided labels.

## 30. Rights, privacy, and release

The TNEP compiler enforces DR-10 before model invocation.

A packet supplied to a hosted or external model may contain only material authorized for that provider, purpose, and retention policy.

Restricted, user-private, or holdout evidence may require:

- local execution;
- redaction;
- metadata-only representation;
- reviewed evidence packets;
- no-retention endpoints;
- complete exclusion.

Model outputs inherit the applicable lineage until a separate rights review concludes otherwise.

No learned component may be released without its own artifact rights manifest and extraction evaluation.

## 31. Failure and recovery behavior

### Implementation failure

If code or configuration does not conform to the approved contract, Sol repairs it under the same design and returns it for review before execution continues.

### Evidence failure

If the necessary evidence is missing, the system returns `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE` or a scoped answer rather than redesigning the experiment or inventing support.

### Model failure

A schema, citation, or deterministic validation failure triggers bounded retry or escalation under a frozen policy.

### Component failure

A stale, incompatible, unavailable, or failed auxiliary head, adapter, graph memory, specialist, or router is surfaced explicitly. The system may fall back only to a preapproved parent path. It may not silently attach a different component or claim that the fallback used the failed capability.

Every modular tier defines:

```text
component-off behavior
component-on behavior
fallback behavior
parent-equivalence tolerance
rollback artifact
compatibility contract
```

### Design failure

If the approved architecture cannot represent a required case, Sol returns:

```text
BLOCKED_REQUIRES_DESIGN_REVIEW
```

Sol may propose options but may not implement a material design change without a new approved review.

### Campaign failure

Luna stops the frozen run and returns evidence to the parent Sol turn. Luna may not modify code, packet schemas, model configuration, data mixture, or experimental design.

## 32. Hard failures

The following are hard failures when material:

- Allowing model output to mutate authoritative TNC records without promotion.
- Treating the model's confidence as scholarly authority.
- Producing a diagnosis without exact source and target identities.
- Citing evidence IDs absent from the packet.
- Hiding a required missing-evidence state.
- Reversing causal order.
- Treating alignment as equivalence.
- Treating translation frequency as witness count.
- Inferring translator intent from effect alone.
- Inferring confessional influence from identity alone.
- Converting an ancient version into exact source wording without DR-08 support.
- Losing alternative diagnoses during packet compilation.
- Hiding rights-driven omissions.
- Presenting a model-generated translation as published.
- Allowing a sidecar embedding or learned head to replace the authoritative graph.
- Reporting an architecture gain without a matched A0/A1 ablation.
- Introducing a custom component that breaks multimodal, multilingual, quantization, or deployment behavior without disclosure.
- Attaching a head, adapter, memory, specialist, or router to an incompatible parent revision.
- Losing source handles, competing analyses, rights masks, or causal order in a tensor or graph projection.
- Allowing a learned router to suppress required Translation Nuance analysis merely to reduce cost.
- Using private holdout or user-private records in training.
- Changing the approved ontology, compatibility matrix, or promotion rules as an implementation detail.

## 34. Binding decisions

Approval of DR-12 would lock the following:

1. The authoritative Translation Nuance record remains external to the foundation model.
2. The project implements a custom `TranslationNuanceSemanticKernel` as a deterministic semantic execution layer, distinct from a GPU kernel or neural block.
3. The semantic kernel enforces identity, evidence, causal, lineage, rights, and completeness invariants but does not adjudicate contested scholarship without an approved rule or reviewed assertion.
4. Every formal model analysis consumes an immutable `TranslationNuanceEvidencePacket`.
5. Every scholarly or benchmark response first produces a typed `TranslationNuanceAnalysisCandidate` and claim ledger.
6. Model-generated alignments, diagnoses, lineage, translations, and claims remain candidates until promoted.
7. Structure-first generation and semantic verification precede final prose in scholarly modes.
8. The TNC graph, tools, packet compiler, model, verifier, and renderer remain distinct logical components.
9. A0 is the mandatory structured-system baseline and may remain the final product architecture if it meets the threshold.
10. A1 preserves foundation topology and is the default learned specialization.
11. A2 auxiliary heads use approved representation taps, preserve DR-07 scholarly spans, and remain training-only by default unless runtime value is demonstrated.
12. The preferred first A3 architecture is a bounded relation-aware residual adapter with deterministic initial routing and an exact off path.
13. The preferred first A4 architecture is a bounded relation-aware graph encoder supplying typed memory through gated cross-attention while the parent model remains independently runnable.
14. A5 specialist systems and explicit compact-to-large routing remain approved alternatives when they improve reliability, auditability, or cost.
15. A6 remains unauthorized without a separate approved design review.
16. Every modular tier defines component-off, fallback, compatibility, parent-equivalence, and rollback behavior.
17. Custom GPU kernels may optimize an approved operation only after profiling and equivalence validation.
18. The same logical TNC contract applies across Qwen, Gemma, Ministral, compact, large, and specialist models, while family-specific implementations are permitted.
19. Multi-model routing uses deterministic case-complexity and validation signals rather than model confidence alone.
20. Multilingual, multimodal, and long-context behavior retain the same evidence and verification requirements.
21. A full-New-Testament context is supplementary and does not replace the TNEP.
22. Every learned or runtime contribution must be isolated through an explicit ablation matrix.
23. Every invocation binds exact model, packet, graph, tool, renderer, validator, rights, and runtime identities.
24. Sol implements the approved architecture; ChatGPT designs and reviews experiments; Joseph Abbud approves consequential changes and releases.
25. Luna may execute only frozen campaigns delegated by Sol and may not alter code, architecture, configuration, data, or experiment design.

## 36. Approved statement

> **Biblical Scholar Lab will integrate Translation Nuance through a layered, evidence-bearing architecture in which the authoritative graph, deterministic Translation Nuance Semantic Kernel, immutable model evidence packet, foundation model, semantic verifier, claim ledger, and answer renderer remain distinct but interoperable components. The model may retrieve, compare, propose, explain, generate bounded translation options, and identify missing evidence, but model-generated alignments, diagnoses, lineage, source assignments, translations, and scholarly claims will remain candidates until explicitly promoted. Every formal analysis will bind exact source and target revisions, linguistic and textual evidence, translation lineage, cause-axis and causal-role structure, scholarly evidence, rights, graph snapshot, model artifact, tools, renderer, and validator. The mandatory A0 baseline will preserve official foundation topology and test the structured system before learned specialization; A1 will add translation-aware objectives without topology change; auxiliary heads, relation-aware adapters, graph memory, specialist models, and core architecture modification will require progressively stronger evidence, controlled ablation, capability-retention analysis, ChatGPT design, and owner approval. A custom semantic execution kernel is approved at the system level. The preferred first learned extensions, if later gated, are a separately ablatable auxiliary-head suite, a bounded relation-aware residual adapter, and a query-scoped graph-memory sidecar with typed memory and a complete disable path. Custom GPU kernels may only optimize profiled operations, and no core foundation-model modification is authorized by this review.**
