## 1. Purpose

DR-20 defines what the benchmark suite must measure and which conditions must remain separate.

DR-21 defines how authoritative benchmark content is created, reviewed, scored, revised, protected, and interpreted.

The benchmark will include exact deterministic cases, structured linguistic and Translation Nuance tasks, open-ended scholarly synthesis, tool trajectories, multilingual and multimodal cases, long-context sessions, safety cases, and human-workflow evaluations. Those cannot all use one annotation method, one gold-answer format, one inter-rater statistic, or one automatic scorer.

A benchmark can appear rigorous while still failing if:

- Its prompt is ambiguous;
- Its gold answer reflects one hidden method or tradition;
- Its evidence packet omits decisive counterevidence;
- Its rubric double-counts one behavior;
- Its annotators lack the relevant specialty;
- Its deterministic scorer validates syntax but not meaning;
- Its LLM judge rewards verbosity or citation density;
- Its disagreement is adjudicated away rather than preserved;
- Its private cases are exposed during case generation or scorer development;
- Its aggregate score hides severe source, citation, safety, language, or modality failures.

DR-21 is intended to prevent those outcomes.

It does **not** yet author the actual benchmark cases. Case blueprints and case batches will be generated by ChatGPT under the approved process, reviewed by Joseph, and specialist-validated where required. Sol will implement the machinery only after these contracts are approved.

## 2. Governing principle

> **An authoritative benchmark case is a versioned scholarly measurement instrument, not merely a prompt and an answer. Every case must identify the construct being measured, exact evidence conditions, accepted and prohibited behaviors, review authority, scoring semantics, dependency and contamination relationships, uncertainty, and rights. Benchmark content will be authored by ChatGPT and approved by Joseph Abbud; specialist gold will remain provisional until qualification-matched human review. Sol may implement the instrument but may not decide what it asks or what constitutes a correct scholarly response.**

The benchmark lifecycle is:

```text
approved track and task-family blueprint
    → ChatGPT-authored case batch
    → owner review and revision
    → source, rights, and deterministic validation
    → ChatGPT methodology review
    → specialist review where required
    → pilot and discrimination analysis
    → immutable case-family and scoring freeze
    → Sol implementation and ingestion
    → frozen evaluation execution
    → result review, incident handling, and versioned release
```

Every benchmark record preserves distinct authority states.

```text

REJECTED

```

These states must never be collapsed.

### 3.1 ChatGPT design authority

ChatGPT owns:

- Benchmark and case-family blueprints;
- Individual prompts and variants;
- Evidence and answer contracts;
- Gold boundaries and accepted alternatives;
- Required uncertainty and prohibited overclaims;
- Atomic semantic rubrics;
- Case-family, dependency, and contamination relationships;
- Split recommendations;
- Fresh challenge construction;
- Benchmark-content amendments and incident repairs.

ChatGPT may use tools, source research, and model-assisted drafting internally, but the final design record must identify ChatGPT as the benchmark designer and preserve any material upstream model-generated origin.

### 3.2 Owner approval

Joseph reviews and approves:

- Product relevance;
- Fairness and clarity;
- Public/private placement;
- Whether the behavior is worth measuring;
- Whether the case is appropriately difficult and useful;
- Whether the benchmark batch should be frozen, revised, or rejected;
- Public claims and releases.

Owner approval authorizes project use within the approved scope. It does not certify specialist philological, textual-critical, theological, historical, linguistic, or pastoral correctness.

### 3.3 Deterministic and source validation

Deterministic validation can establish matters such as:

- Reference validity;
- Exact quotation identity;
- Source span presence;
- Schema conformance;
- Edition and artifact hashes;
- Citation existence;
- Tool-call syntax;
- Rights and split constraints;
- Generator reproducibility.

Source verification confirms that a source says or contains what the case claims it says or contains.

Neither automatically resolves a disputed interpretation.

### 3.4 ChatGPT methodology review

ChatGPT methodology review establishes that the case and rubric conform to the approved epistemic, Translation Nuance, linguistic, citation, safety, multilingual, multimodal, context, and benchmark designs.

It does not masquerade as independent human subject-matter-expert validation.

### 3.5 SME review

`SME_REVIEWED` means that a human with recorded qualifications relevant to the particular judgment has reviewed the applicable case fields.

One person is not automatically qualified across:

- New Testament Greek;
- Biblical Hebrew or Aramaic;
- Septuagint;
- Textual criticism;
- Syriac or Coptic;
- Translation studies;
- Patristics;
- Modern scholarship;
- Native-language output;
- Pastoral or clinical safety.

The review record identifies the exact fields and constructs the reviewer validated.

## 4. Review partitions

The DR-19 and DR-20 partitions govern benchmark authority.

### `REV-P0_DETERMINISTIC_AND_OPERATIONAL`

Appropriate when the gold can be established through exact sources, deterministic policy, or mechanically testable behavior.

Examples:

- Correct passage resolution;
- Exact quotation from an identified edition;
- Valid citation identity;
- Correct tool schema;
- Page-region citation;
- Study-note versus scripture classification;
- Hidden pivot disclosure;
- Rights or privacy compliance;
- Proper handling of an unrelated request.

### `REV-P1_SOURCE_VERIFIABLE_SCHOLARLY_BEHAVIOR`

Appropriate when the expected behavior can be established by reviewed sources and approved methodology without choosing among subtle specialist positions.

Examples:

- Distinguishing translation agreement from manuscript evidence;
- Admitting that a lexicon gloss does not settle contextual meaning;
- Disclosing that only an abstract was accessed;
- Presenting two documented positions without inventing consensus;
- Avoiding certain retroversion from nondiagnostic versional wording;
- Preserving evidence and inference distinctions.

### `REV-P2_SPECIALIST_SCHOLARLY_JUDGMENT`

Required when correctness depends materially on specialist judgment.

Examples:

- Selecting the strongest of disputed syntactic analyses;
- Judging which textual reading is earlier;
- Assessing whether a version reflects another Vorlage;
- Determining whether a translation reflects confessional influence;
- Evaluating a disputed intertextual relationship;
- Characterizing a current scholarly consensus;
- Assessing tradition-specific balance.

A case may be reclassified upward when review reveals hidden specialist dependence.

## 8. Case-batch process

Case content should be reviewed in bounded batches.

### Initial batch size

The default owner-review batch is:

```text
10–25 case families or cases
```

For complex `REV-P2`, multimodal, or multi-turn cases, a batch may be:

```text
5–10 cases
```

The purpose is to catch conceptual defects before they propagate.

### Required batch artifacts

Each batch includes:

- Batch identity and hash;
- Approved family blueprints;
- Cases and variants;
- Source and rights manifest;
- Review-partition summary;
- Deterministic validation report;
- Known questions and uncertainties;
- Public/private recommendations;
- Expected SME queue;
- Change log from any previous owner review.

### Owner disposition

Joseph may return:

```text
APPROVE_BATCH
APPROVE_WITH_EXPLICIT_LIMITATIONS
REVISION_REQUIRED
SPLIT_REQUIRED
REJECT_BATCH
SME_REVIEW_REQUIRED_BEFORE_APPROVAL
```

ChatGPT—not Sol—makes the requested content revisions.

## 9. Case authoring requirements

Every case must be self-contained within its declared mode and must not rely on hidden information available only to its author.

It records:

```text
user-facing prompt
system or runtime conditions
answer-depth mode
language and locale
canon and edition context
modality
available tools
available evidence
withheld evidence
expected clarification behavior
currentness horizon
rights/display limitations
```

A case should specify what it actually measures.

For example, a question about Romans 3:22 may measure:

- Greek genitive interpretation;
- Translation comparison;
- Scholarly-landscape synthesis;
- Tool selection;
- Citation fidelity;

Those are distinct constructs and should not be bundled accidentally into one opaque score.

## 10. Evidence contracts

Every consequential case receives an `EvidenceContract` specifying:

```text
required evidence
permitted evidence
withheld evidence
irrelevant distractors
material counterevidence
source identity and revision
claim types the evidence can support
claim types it cannot support
access and display rights
evidence insufficiency state
expected abstention or qualification
```

### Evidence completeness

The case must disclose whether its evidence packet is:

```text
COMPLETE_FOR_DEFINED_CLAIM
SUFFICIENT_WITH_QUALIFICATION
INTENTIONALLY_INCOMPLETE
CONFLICTED
RIGHTS_REDACTED
TOOL_DEPENDENT
LIVE_RETRIEVAL_REQUIRED
```

A model cannot be penalized for failing to establish a claim that the benchmark itself did not provide enough evidence to establish.

### Counterevidence

If a case tests balanced synthesis, its contract must identify material counterevidence or state why none is required.

## 11. Answer contracts

The authoritative gold is ordinarily a structured boundary, not a model answer paragraph.

An `AnswerContract` may include:

```text
required claims
conditionally required claims
accepted alternative claims
accepted analysis sets
required evidence links
required counterevidence
required uncertainty
required tool actions
required source distinctions
required answer language
required answer-depth behavior
acceptable abstention conditions
prohibited claims
prohibited source confusions
hard failures
```

### Accepted analysis sets

A disputed case may preserve several accepted analyses, each with:

```text
conditions
method or perspective
required supporting evidence
required qualifications
counterarguments
prohibited overclaims
review authority
```

A response is not penalized merely for selecting one defensible analysis when its method and limitations are properly disclosed.

### No reference-answer imitation requirement

The benchmark does not require wording similarity to a reference answer unless the task explicitly measures exact text.

## 12. Atomic rubric contract

Open-ended cases are scored through atomic criteria rather than one holistic impression.

Every `AtomicCriterion` records:

```text
criterion_id
construct
description
applicability condition
scoring type
scale and anchors
maximum points or weight
severity
hard-failure relation
prerequisites and dependencies
accepted evidence
prohibited shortcuts
review authority
scorer types allowed
```

### Criterion types

```text
EXACT_BOOLEAN
EXACT_CATEGORICAL
SET_OR_MULTI_LABEL
SPAN_OR_ALIGNMENT
ORDERED_SEQUENCE
GRAPH_RELATION
ORDINAL_RUBRIC
CONTINUOUS_ERROR
CALIBRATION
ABSTENTION
TOOL_TRAJECTORY
MULTIMODAL_GROUNDING
SAFETY_OR_POLICY
EFFICIENCY
```

### Default qualitative scale

Where an ordinal scale is necessary, the default is:

```text
0 = absent, incorrect, or materially unsupported
1 = partially correct but materially incomplete or weakly supported
2 = correct and sufficiently supported for the defined case
```

A four-level scale may be used when the anchors are meaningfully distinct. Vague 1–10 ratings are prohibited.

### Rubric atomicity

Each criterion should measure one behavior where practical.

The benchmark must audit for:

- Double counting;
- Hidden dependency;
- Style criteria standing in for accuracy;
- Criteria impossible to score from available evidence;
- Criteria whose anchors overlap;
- Criteria that reward only one defensible methodology.

Expert-authored, fine-grained rubrics have been shown to reveal important capability differences that aggregate impressions miss, while recent rubric-evaluation work finds a substantial gap between human-annotated and autonomously model-generated rubrics.[^prbench][^rubricbench]

## 13. Criterion dependencies and upstream failure

Criteria can depend on one another.

For example:

```text
correct passage identity
    → correct source-text analysis
    → correct Translation Nuance diagnosis
```

The rubric represents this dependency explicitly.

When an upstream prerequisite fails, downstream criteria may be marked:

```text
NOT_EVALUABLE_DUE_TO_UPSTREAM_FAILURE
```

rather than receiving misleading independent zeros.

The case may still apply an approved score cap or hard failure for the upstream defect.

This preserves diagnostic value while avoiding double punishment.

## 14. Hard failures and severity

Every case may define hard failures independent of weighted criteria.

Severity classes are:

```text
HF-1_CRITICAL
HF-2_MAJOR
HF-3_MATERIAL
HF-4_MINOR
```

Examples:

### `HF-1_CRITICAL`

- Harmful spiritual-authority instruction;
- Restricted or private data leakage;
- Fabricated source central to a conclusion;
- Claiming an illegible page region was read;
- Direct instruction to harm under purported divine authority.

### `HF-2_MAJOR`

- Modern translations counted as manuscript witnesses;
- Study note presented as scripture;
- Certain retroversion from underdetermined evidence;
- Real citation attached to a central unsupported claim;
- Hidden language pivot materially affecting the analysis.

A hard failure can:

- Zero a criterion;
- Cap a case score;
- Cap a track score;
- Block product promotion;

according to the frozen `HardFailureRule`.

One aggregate score may never average away a hard failure.

## 15. Deterministic scoring

Deterministic scorers are preferred where the construct allows them.

They may score:

- Reference and edition identity;
- Exact quotations;
- Source spans and locators;
- Tool calls and schemas;
- Structured identifiers;
- Rights and split compliance;
- Citation existence and publication status;
- Page-region selections;
- Alignment overlap;
- Multi-label cause sets;
- Ordered causal chains;
- Evidence-handle use;
- Answer-language identity;
- Prohibited phrase or authority patterns where semantically safe.

Every deterministic scorer receives:

- A versioned specification;
- Positive and negative fixtures authored under the benchmark-design process;
- Boundary and adversarial fixtures;
- Unit and property tests;
- A false-positive and false-negative audit;
- A scorer content hash.

A passing schema is not proof of substantive correctness.

## 16. Structured scoring

### Multi-label and set-valued targets

The benchmark may report:

- Precision;
- Recall;
- F1;
- Exact-set match;
- Hierarchy-aware credit;
- Required-label recall;
- Prohibited-label rate.

Translation Nuance cause sets must account for the approved ontology and causal roles.

### Span and alignment targets

Possible metrics include:

- Character, grapheme, token, word, or scholarly-span overlap;
- Source and target precision/recall;
- Link precision/recall;
- Many-to-many alignment F1;
- Null-alignment accuracy;
- Discontinuous-span accuracy.

The coordinate system and segmentation revision must be explicit.

### Ordered chains

Causal-chain scoring can separately report:

- Correct causes;
- Correct causal roles;
- Correct order;
- Missing upstream causes;
- Spurious causes;
- Unsupported certainty.

### Graph relations

Graph scoring distinguishes node identity, edge type, direction, passage scope, evidence, and confidence.

A graph edge with the wrong direction does not receive full credit merely because the same entities appear.

## 17. Open-ended rubric scoring

Open-ended scoring uses the atomic criteria and evidence contract.

The scorer must evaluate:

- Whether the claimed content is present;
- Whether it is supported;
- Whether material qualifications are present;
- Whether counterevidence is handled;
- Whether the answer exceeds what the evidence permits;
- Whether citations and quotations attach to the correct claims;
- Whether answer depth and language are appropriate.

Surface polish, length, academic tone, citation density, and Greek or Hebrew terminology do not receive accuracy credit unless the case explicitly measures them.

Span-based and criterion-specific rubrics have shown better alignment with expert scoring than broad holistic judge prompts in open-ended professional evaluations.[^greekbar]

## 18. Partial credit

Partial credit is allowed only when its semantics are defined in advance.

Examples:

- Correctly identifies syntactic ambiguity but omits one material alternative;
- Correctly distinguishes translation choice from textual variant but gives weak evidence;
- Selects the correct source but provides an imprecise locator;
- Correctly abstains but fails to explain the evidence limitation.

Partial credit may not turn a central fabrication or source-type confusion into a passing answer.

## 19. Abstention and selective prediction

Abstention is a first-class answer behavior.

The benchmark distinguishes:

```text
APPROPRIATE_ABSTENTION
OVER_ABSTENTION
UNDER_ABSTENTION
PARTIAL_ABSTENTION
QUALIFIED_ANSWER
UNSUPPORTED_CONFIDENCE
```

Metrics may include:

- Accuracy at coverage;
- Selective risk;
- Risk-coverage curves;
- Area under the risk-coverage curve;
- Abstention precision and recall;
- Utility under an approved error/abstention cost matrix.

A model is not rewarded for refusing every hard case.

## 20. Confidence and calibration

Confidence should be claim-specific where possible.

The benchmark may use:

- Brier score;
- Log loss;
- Reliability diagrams;
- Expected calibration error as a diagnostic;
- Class-conditional calibration;
- Risk-coverage analysis;
- Calibration by language, modality, task, and review partition.

No one scalar calibration statistic is sufficient.

A model that is calibrated overall but systematically overconfident on Greek, Hebrew, multimodal, or textual-critical cases fails the relevant subgroup requirement.

## 21. Human review assignment

A `ReviewerProfile` records:

```text
reviewer identity or privacy-safe pseudonym
qualifications
languages and scripts
specialties
traditions or methodological perspectives, where relevant
review roles authorized
conflicts of interest
compensation or relationship
training and calibration completed
review history
```

The system does not infer expertise from institutional affiliation alone.

### Conflict disclosure

A conflict does not always disqualify a reviewer, but it must be disclosed.

Examples include:

- Authorship of a source under evaluation;
- Employment by a model vendor;
- Participation in the model or benchmark team;
- Strong public advocacy for a disputed position;
- Financial or publication interest.

A conflicted reviewer should not be the sole adjudicator of a consequential disputed case.

## 22. Reviewer calibration

Before authoritative annotation, reviewers complete a bounded calibration set containing:

- Clear cases;
- Common boundary errors;
- Legitimate pluralism;
- Insufficient-evidence cases;
- Hard-failure examples;
- Track-specific rubric anchors.

Calibration is intended to clarify the rubric, not force reviewers into one theological or methodological conclusion.

Repeated confusion about an item may indicate a defective case or rubric rather than a defective reviewer.

## 23. Blinding

Where feasible, human and model-based evaluators are blinded to:

- Model and vendor identity;
- Training lineage;
- Whether the answer is a baseline or candidate;
- Response order in pairwise comparisons;
- Project preference for a particular architecture.

Response order is randomized.

Metadata necessary for correctness—such as cited edition, language, evidence mode, or system tools—remains visible.

The evaluation must not blind reviewers to information needed to judge the claim.

## 24. `REV-P0` validation protocol

Authoritative `REV-P0` gold normally requires:

1. ChatGPT-designed case and answer contract;
2. Joseph owner approval;
3. Deterministic validation;
4. Source verification where applicable;
5. ChatGPT methodology review;
6. Pilot against at least one known-correct and one known-incorrect fixture or system output;
7. Immutable freeze.

A deterministic case should achieve exact reviewer/scorer agreement after defects are repaired.

## 25. `REV-P1` validation protocol

Authoritative `REV-P1` gold normally requires:

1. ChatGPT design;
2. Owner approval;
3. Source verification;
4. Explicit methodology review;
5. Review of material alternatives and prohibited overclaims;
6. Pilot against several system outputs;
7. Adjudication of discovered ambiguity;
8. Frozen limitations.

Qualified human review is encouraged and may become mandatory for a track or public claim, but `REV-P1` does not claim specialist validation unless it actually occurred.

## 26. `REV-P2` validation protocol

### Clear specialist technical cases

A narrowly scoped specialist case may become `SME_REVIEWED` with:

- One qualification-matched primary SME;
- Independent source verification;
- ChatGPT methodology review;
- Owner approval;
- A documented spot-check plan across the case family.

### Contested, high-consequence, or promotion-critical cases

These ordinarily require:

- At least two independent qualification-matched SMEs;
- Blinded initial review where feasible;
- Preservation of pre-adjudication judgments;
- Adjudication by agreement, a third qualified reviewer, or a plural accepted-answer contract;
- Disclosure of remaining disagreement.

A majority vote does not automatically establish scholarly truth.

### Perspective-conditioned cases

Cases concerning confessional or methodological interpretation should include reviewers competent in the relevant perspectives and should preserve conditional correctness rather than forcing one unmarked answer.

## 28. Inter-rater agreement and reliability

Agreement is reported at the **criterion level** and case-family level where appropriate, not only on total scores.

The default report includes:

- Number and qualifications of reviewers;
- Raw agreement;
- Confusion matrix or rating distribution;
- Krippendorff's alpha with the appropriate distance function and uncertainty interval;
- A prevalence-robust diagnostic such as Gwet's AC1 or AC2 where category imbalance makes kappa-like measures difficult to interpret;
- Reviewer and case-family variance;
- Adjudication rate;
- Distribution of disagreement types.

Krippendorff's alpha is useful because it supports multiple reviewers, several levels of measurement, and missing judgments, but reliability coefficients must be interpreted with their assumptions rather than treated as automatic validity certificates.[^krippendorff][^hayes]

### Initial authority thresholds

The default targets are:

```text
alpha >= 0.80
    eligible for authoritative use, subject to validity and hard-failure review

0.67 <= alpha < 0.80
    provisional use only, with adjudication, limitation reporting,
    and no unsupported high-stakes claim

alpha < 0.67
    not authoritative until the case, rubric, reviewer training,
    or accepted-pluralism contract is revised
```

These are starting governance thresholds—not universal natural laws.

### Legitimate disagreement

Low agreement may reveal:

- A defective prompt;
- An unclear rubric;
- Insufficient reviewer training;
- Hidden specialist complexity;
- Genuine scholarly pluralism.

The project must diagnose which condition applies.

Genuine pluralism should be represented as multiple accepted analyses, not “repaired” into artificial unanimity.

## 29. Model-assisted scoring is secondary

LLM judges may assist with:

- Triage;
- Criterion pre-scoring;
- Disagreement detection;
- Missing-citation detection;
- Candidate error categorization;
- Public smoke evaluations.

They may not independently create gold, validate `REV-P2`, or control promotion without track-specific calibration.

Large empirical studies find that LLM judge reliability varies substantially by task, property, expertise requirement, and input source.[^judgebench] Contextual judge benchmarks also show that supplied evidence and complex criteria remain difficult for strong judge models.[^contextjudge]

## 30. Judge calibration records

Every allowed judge configuration receives a `JudgeCalibrationRecord` binding:

```text
judge model and exact revision
system and judge prompt
reasoning mode
sampling and repetition policy
rubric and case revision
track, language, modality, and evidence mode
human comparison set
position and length-bias tests
intra-judge consistency
criterion-level performance
hard-failure recall
known limitations
expiration and refresh conditions
```

A judge validated on English text cannot automatically score:

- Spanish;
- Greek;
- Hebrew;
- Page images;
- Textual criticism;
- Sensitive-use cases.

## 31. Minimum judge gates

The following initial gates apply before a model judge can be used as a **primary automated scorer** for an ordinary non-SME criterion:

```text
human-scored calibration set >= 100 response-criterion judgments
criterion-level macro F1 or balanced accuracy >= 0.85
raw agreement with adjudicated human labels >= 0.85
hard-failure recall >= 0.95 where hard failures are in scope
no material worst-group result below its approved threshold
position-order and length-bias tests passed
intra-judge consistency documented
```

These are minimum gates, not guarantees of validity.

A judge failing them may still be used for triage when clearly labeled and excluded from authoritative scores.

For `REV-P2`, human SME scoring remains authoritative even if a judge passes ordinary criteria.

## 32. Judge-bias controls

The calibration suite tests:

- Response-order reversal;
- Length matching;
- Citation-density bias;
- Academic-tone bias;
- Model/vendor identity masking;
- Self-preference;
- Agreement with reference wording despite unsupported content;
- Excessive confidence;
- Multilingual and modality bias;
- Sensitivity to irrelevant context;
- Repeat-run consistency.

LLM judges can show substantial run-to-run inconsistency, so repeated or deterministic judge execution and variance reporting are required where one run is not stable.[^roulette]

## 33. Scorer hierarchy

The authoritative scoring order is:

```text
1. Deterministic exact scorer
2. Deterministic structured scorer
3. Source-grounded criterion scorer
4. Qualification-matched human reviewer
5. Calibrated model-assisted scorer
6. Uncalibrated model triage only
```

A lower layer does not override a higher authoritative layer merely because it is cheaper or faster.

## 34. Case pilot and discrimination

Before a case controls promotion, it should be piloted on a diverse set of outputs, such as:

- Known-correct fixtures;
- Known-hard-failure fixtures;
- Foundation model alone;
- Model plus tools/RAG;
- Complete A0 runtime;
- Compact and large candidate models;
- Human reference outputs where available.

The pilot records:

```text
completion and timeout rate
score distribution
hard-failure distribution
criterion discrimination
case-family robustness
reviewer disagreement
scorer disagreement
unexpected shortcuts
language/modality gaps
cost and latency
```

A case may be classified:

```text
VALID_AND_DISCRIMINATIVE
VALID_REGRESSION_SENTINEL
TOO_EASY_FOR_PRIMARY_CLAIMS
TOO_HARD_OR_EVIDENCE_INSUFFICIENT
NONDISCRIMINATING
AMBIGUOUS
SCORER_DEFECT
LEAKAGE_RISK
RIGHTS_BLOCKED
REQUIRES_SME
REJECTED
```

Easy cases may remain valuable regression sentinels. They should not dominate primary capability claims.

## 35. Difficulty and item statistics

After enough results exist, the project may estimate:

- Pass rate;
- Criterion difficulty;
- Item-total or track discrimination;
- Case-family worst-case performance;
- Human-model gap;
- Floor and ceiling effects;
- Response-time and evidence-use burden.

Item-response models may be explored later, but no latent difficulty estimate may replace content review.

Cases will not be selected merely because they make the preferred model rank higher.

## 36. Benchmark size envelopes

DR-21 sets planning envelopes rather than a fixed count.

### `MVP-01_EXPERT_COLLABORATION_PREVIEW`

Target:

```text
100–160 case families
180–300 executable variants
```

The preview should be dominated by authoritative `REV-P0` and reviewed `REV-P1`, with selected `REV-P2` cases published only as `SME_REVIEW_PENDING` collaboration candidates.

Translation Nuance should receive the largest individual share of cases, but no public claim is made for a track lacking enough independent case families to be interpretable.

### Initial internal `BSL_BENCHMARK_SUITE` v0.1 target

After expert collaboration begins:

```text
240–400 case families
450–800 executable variants
```

This is an envelope, not an instruction to fill every track mechanically. Quality, independence, and review authority outrank count.

### Minimum reportability floor

A track should ordinarily have at least:

```text
15 independent case families
```

before a stable aggregate is emphasized.

The signature Translation Nuance track should ordinarily reach at least:

```text
30 independent case families
```

before a major model-family or training claim is made.

Small specialist tracks may still be reported as exploratory with explicit uncertainty.

## 37. Initial partition envelopes

The initial suite should target, by **case family**, approximately:

```text
35–45% public development and reproduction
55–65% private audit, selection, and final evaluation
```

Within the private portion, selection and final sets remain separate.

The fresh post-freeze challenge is created later and should add approximately:

```text
10–20% of the then-current authoritative suite size
```

as genuinely new case families.

These are planning ranges. Relationship clusters, rights, scarcity, and SME availability can require deviation, which must be approved and reported.

## 38. Statistical unit and cluster awareness

The default independent analysis unit is the `case_family_id` or another declared relationship cluster—not every prompt variant.

The benchmark reports both:

- Variant-level performance for diagnostics;
- Cluster-level performance for statistical claims.

Translation, paraphrase, language, page-degradation, and multi-turn variants cannot be counted as fully independent observations.

## 39. Aggregate reporting

Primary reports include:

- Macro average across case families;
- Per-track macro results;
- Per-language and per-modality results;
- Worst-group and worst-family results;
- Hard-failure counts and rates;
- Calibration and abstention;
- Cost and latency;
- Reviewer/scorer uncertainty.

Micro averages may be reported as secondary diagnostics.

No aggregate overrides the DR-20 hard-failure or promotion policy.

## 40. Comparative statistics

Model and system comparisons should use paired, cluster-aware methods.

The default is:

- Paired differences at case-family level;
- Cluster bootstrap confidence intervals;
- At least 10,000 bootstrap resamples for final reports unless an exact or approved alternative is more suitable;
- Practical effect size;
- Raw counts and denominators;
- Treatment of timeouts, failures, and missing outputs;
- Repeated-run variance where stochasticity is material.

For several preregistered primary comparisons, the report uses an approved multiple-comparison control, initially Holm's step-down procedure unless a later statistical design selects another method.

Statistical significance alone does not establish practical or scholarly importance.

## 41. Missing, failed, and blocked runs

A failed generation, timeout, tool error, rights block, or unsupported modality is not silently dropped.

The result is classified:

```text
MODEL_FAILURE
RUNTIME_FAILURE
TOOL_FAILURE
RIGHTS_BLOCKED
EVIDENCE_UNAVAILABLE
SCORER_FAILURE
TIMEOUT
CANCELLED
NOT_APPLICABLE
```

Reports show:

- Completion rate;
- Failure cause;
- Whether the failure counts against the evaluated system under the frozen contract;
- Sensitivity analyses where appropriate.

## 42. Benchmark-generation security

Private and fresh benchmark content must be generated and stored through an approved private workflow.

Requirements include:

- No public repository commits;
- Least-privilege access;
- No ordinary training or synthesis agents;
- No sending private cases to the system being evaluated;
- Separate keys and storage from public cases;
- Immutable access logs;
- No use in prompt, retrieval, or scorer tuning;
- Rights and privacy review before storage and execution.

### Author-model contamination

Every case records whether a generative model assisted its creation.

A case generated or materially shaped by a model may be used to evaluate other model families under the recorded conditions, but it cannot support an uncontaminated claim about the same model or provider without a separately constructed human/SME fresh subset.

Because ChatGPT is the benchmark designer, the OpenAI frontier-ceiling scorecard must include a distinct human- or SME-authored fresh subset for any claim intended to be independent of ChatGPT-authored case exposure.

## 43. Deterministic case generation

Sol may implement approved generators for:

- Synthetic page layouts;
- OCR perturbations;
- Reference aliases;
- Canon or versification variants;
- Controlled distractor insertion;
- Case-order or response-order randomization;
- Approved multilingual rendering where exact source translations are supplied;
- Metamorphic variants.

The generator contract must freeze:

```text
input records
transformation policy
random seed and bounds
expected invariants
expected gold-generation rule
case-family identity
split inheritance
rights and privacy behavior
```

A generated variant is rejected if its transformation changes the intended answer without updating the answer contract.

## 44. Model-generated case candidates

A model may propose:

- Potential errors;
- Distractors;
- Paraphrases;
- Adversarial prompts;
- Candidate translations;
- Candidate rubric gaps.

Those remain:

```text
MODEL_GENERATED_CANDIDATE
```

They cannot become authoritative without:

- ChatGPT design adoption or rewrite;
- Owner approval;
- Source and methodology review;
- Applicable SME validation;
- Contamination and author-model analysis;
- A new content hash.

RubricBench reports a meaningful gap between human-annotated and autonomously model-generated rubrics, reinforcing that model suggestions are useful candidates rather than final authority.[^rubricbench]

## 45. Annotation workbench contract

Sol will later implement an annotation and review workbench that supports:

- Immutable case and evidence revisions;
- Side-by-side diffs;
- Role-based access;
- Blind output review;
- Source-span inspection;
- Criterion-level annotation;
- Reviewer confidence and rationale;
- Conflict disclosure;
- Independent review before adjudication;
- Preservation of pre-adjudication labels;
- Owner disposition;
- Batch hashing and freeze;
- Public/private export;
- Incident and supersession workflows.

The workbench must not contain a “last edit wins” path for gold or rubric changes.

Collaborative, iterative engagement can improve annotation quality for nuanced constructs compared with isolated anonymous rating, but the process must preserve independent initial judgments before group discussion.[^annotatorloop]

## 46. Scorer implementation authority

Sol may implement the exact scoring semantics approved here and in case records.

It may choose design-neutral algorithms and libraries for:

- Set and span metrics;
- Reliability statistics;
- Bootstrap intervals;
- Report rendering;
- Storage and computation;

provided they reproduce approved fixtures and do not change the semantic meaning of a score.

Sol may not:

- Invent rubric criteria;
- Change criterion weights or severity;
- Convert an accepted alternative into an error;
- Replace a hard failure with partial credit;
- Select judge prompts or models without an approved calibration design;
- Change the statistical unit;
- Exclude failed runs to improve performance.

Any conflict or ambiguity returns:

```text
BLOCKED_REQUIRES_BENCHMARK_DESIGN_REVIEW
```

## 47. Luna execution authority

Luna may execute only frozen evaluation and scoring campaigns delegated by Sol.

It may:

- Verify exact case, scorer, judge, split, system, and campaign hashes;
- Run approved evaluations;
- Collect outputs and timing;
- Invoke approved deterministic and calibrated scorers;
- Stop at budget and runtime bounds;
- Return objective artifacts to Sol.

It may not:

- See private cases beyond the exact execution grant;
- Author or modify cases;
- Change rubrics, scorers, judge prompts, thresholds, or splits;
- Adjudicate disagreement;
- Interpret or publish results.

## 48. Benchmark incidents

An incident includes:

- Wrong source or gold;
- Ambiguous prompt;
- Invalid accepted alternative;
- Misweighted criterion;
- Broken deterministic scorer;
- Unreliable model judge;
- Reviewer conflict;
- Leakage or exposure;
- Rights or privacy problem;
- Author-model contamination;
- Incorrect split or cluster;
- Statistical error;
- Published claim affected by a case defect.

The incident process is:

```text
freeze affected case and use
→ preserve original records
→ determine scope and affected runs
→ correct, reclassify, split, retire, or supersede
→ version benchmark and scorer
→ rerun affected baselines where material
→ publish correction if public claims changed
```

## 49. `MVP-01_EXPERT_COLLABORATION_PREVIEW` annotation gate

The collaboration preview requires:

- Authoritative public-safe `REV-P0` cases;
- Source-verified and methodology-reviewed `REV-P1` cases;
- Owner approval of every released case batch;
- Public scorer fixtures and reproducibility;
- Clear labels on `SME_REVIEW_PENDING` candidates;
- Benchmark and evaluation cards;
- Known error and disagreement report;
- Specialist contribution queues;
- No use of provisional `REV-P2` as authoritative performance gold.

The preview should demonstrate both what the system can already measure and where expert participation will improve the benchmark.

## 50. Principal hard failures

DR-21 treats the following as hard failures:

- Allowing Sol or Luna to author or materially alter benchmark content.
- Treating owner approval or ChatGPT methodology review as SME validation.
- Treating a model-generated rubric or gold as authoritative without the approved review path.
- Omitting the exact source and evidence contract for a consequential case.
- Forcing one answer where several analyses are defensible.
- Adjudicating legitimate scholarly disagreement into false unanimity.
- Allowing a conflicted reviewer to be the sole authority on a consequential disputed case.
- Using vague holistic scores where atomic criteria are possible.
- Double-counting one error through dependent criteria.
- Allowing partial credit to hide a central fabrication or source-type failure.
- Treating schema-valid output as substantively correct.
- Using an uncalibrated LLM judge as an authoritative scorer.
- Applying an English-text judge validation to another language or modality.
- Hiding judge position, length, style, or self-preference bias.
- Dropping timeouts or failures from the denominator without disclosure.
- Treating prompt variants in one case family as independent samples.
- Creating private final cases through a route accessible to the evaluated system.
- Using ChatGPT-authored cases as uncontaminated evidence of ChatGPT frontier-ceiling performance without a distinct human/SME fresh subset.
- Changing prompt, evidence, gold, rubric, hard-failure, or split semantics without a case revision and impact analysis.
- Publishing private, restricted, or provisional specialist content as authoritative gold.

## 51. Binding decisions

Approval of DR-21 would lock:

1. ChatGPT designs and generates benchmark content; Joseph reviews and approves it; qualification-matched SMEs validate specialist gold.
2. Sol implements benchmark machinery only and cannot author or materially change benchmark content.
3. Distinct design, owner, deterministic, source, methodology, and SME review states remain visible.
4. Case-family blueprints precede case generation.
5. Cases are reviewed in bounded batches, ordinarily 10–25 and 5–10 for complex specialist work.
6. Every case binds exact evidence and answer contracts.
7. Gold is ordinarily a structured boundary with accepted alternatives, required qualifications, and prohibited overclaims.
8. Open-ended scoring uses atomic criteria with explicit applicability, anchors, severity, dependencies, and hard-failure relations.
9. Criterion dependencies prevent double punishment while retaining upstream score caps.
10. Hard failures remain independent of weighted partial credit.
11. Deterministic and structured scoring precede human or model judgment.
12. Abstention and calibration are first-class, claim-aware metrics.
13. `REV-P0`, `REV-P1`, and `REV-P2` receive distinct validation protocols.
14. Consequential contested `REV-P2` cases ordinarily require at least two independent SMEs or preserved plural accepted analyses.
15. Pre-adjudication judgments and disagreement are immutable.
16. Reliability is reported at criterion level using raw agreement, Krippendorff's alpha, and appropriate diagnostics rather than one coefficient alone.
17. `alpha >= 0.80` is the initial authoritative target; `0.67–0.80` is provisional; lower agreement blocks authority unless legitimate pluralism is encoded.
18. LLM judges are secondary and require track-, language-, modality-, and criterion-specific meta-evaluation.
19. A primary automated judge initially requires at least 100 human-scored response-criterion judgments, at least 0.85 criterion-level macro F1 or balanced accuracy, at least 0.85 raw agreement, and at least 0.95 hard-failure recall where applicable.
20. Judge order, length, style, identity, context, and repeatability biases are tested.
21. The default independent statistical unit is the case family or declared relationship cluster.
22. Final comparisons use paired cluster-aware confidence intervals, effect sizes, and complete failure accounting.
23. Case pilots measure discrimination, ambiguity, shortcuts, scorer validity, and cost before cases control promotion.
24. The collaboration-preview planning envelope is 100–160 case families and 180–300 variants.
25. The initial internal v0.1 planning envelope is 240–400 case families and 450–800 variants.
26. Public material initially targets 35–45% of case families; private material 55–65%; fresh post-freeze cases add roughly 10–20% later.
27. Private and fresh construction uses least-privilege workflows and records author-model contamination.
28. OpenAI frontier-ceiling claims require a distinct human- or SME-authored fresh subset rather than relying only on ChatGPT-authored cases.
29. Sol may implement only fully approved deterministic generators with frozen inputs, transformations, invariants, gold rules, and split inheritance.
30. Model-generated cases and rubrics remain candidates until ChatGPT design adoption, owner approval, and applicable expert review.
31. The annotation workbench preserves immutable revisions, independent judgments, adjudication history, access boundaries, and batch hashes.
32. Incidents create versioned corrections and reruns rather than silent gold edits.
33. The collaboration preview releases only public-safe authoritative `REV-P0`/`REV-P1` and explicitly provisional SME candidates.
34. Luna only executes frozen scoring campaigns; ChatGPT retains interpretation and benchmark-design authority; Joseph retains promotion and release authority.

## 53. Proposed approval statement

Case-family blueprints will precede generation, cases will be reviewed in bounded batches, and gold will normally be a structured boundary of required claims, accepted alternatives, evidence, counterevidence, uncertainty, tool behavior, prohibited claims, and hard failures rather than one reference paragraph. Open-ended scoring will use atomic, dependency-aware criteria with explicit anchors, partial-credit semantics, severity, and hard-failure caps; exact and structured scorers will precede human and model judgment; and schema validity, citation existence, fluency, verbosity, academic tone, or translation frequency will never substitute for substantive correctness. `REV-P0`, `REV-P1`, and `REV-P2` will follow distinct validation protocols, while pre-adjudication judgments, reviewer qualifications, conflicts, legitimate pluralism, and remaining disagreement remain visible. LLM judges will remain secondary and will require track-, language-, modality-, and criterion-specific calibration against human labels, position and length-bias tests, repeated-run reliability, worst-group reporting, and high hard-failure recall before authoritative use; `REV-P2` specialist judgment will remain human-governed. Statistical claims will use case-family or relationship-cluster units, paired cluster-aware confidence intervals, practical effect sizes, complete timeout and failure accounting, and explicit multiple-comparison policy. The collaboration preview will target approximately 100–160 case families and 180–300 variants, while the initial internal v0.1 suite will target 240–400 case families and 450–800 variants, with public/private/fresh partitions preserved through least-privilege construction and immutable access records. ChatGPT- or other model-assisted content will retain its origin and cannot support an uncontaminated same-model claim without a distinct human- or SME-authored fresh subset. Sol may not author, rewrite, relabel, semantically score, repartition, or repair benchmark content; any ambiguity or proposed content change must stop with `BLOCKED_REQUIRES_BENCHMARK_DESIGN_REVIEW`.
